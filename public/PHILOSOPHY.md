## 1. Picking and Sun Withering
The master accurately controls the temperature and humidity and picks in the morning, when there is no dew. After picking, the tea leaves are sent to the sun-withering field immediately. Sunlight plays a decisive role in this stage, as natural sunlight can reduce the moisture content of the tea by 8-15%. The master flips the leaves from time to time to ensure that each piece of tea is adequately cared for.

## 2. Indoor Withering
In a constant temperature and humidity indoor environment, the tea is allowed to ferment steadily. It is constantly stirred to promote the action of enzymes and polyphenols, remove the bitterness and raw taste, and control the degree of fermentation. The tea is then left to rest for another 8-10 hours. This gives it a special aroma and taste.

## 3. Frying and Rolling
The tea is placed in a frying furnace at 300-350℃ and stir-fried for 6-8 minutes, which begins the initial rolling and drying and fixes the flavor. The master's frying usually takes a whole night to finish. Each tea leaf must then be rolled 32 times on average to form the unique ball shape of Taiwanese tea. This also allows the tea to ferment again, making the fragrance and aftertaste stronger.

## 4. Baking and Drying
The tea is dried at 110-120℃ to remove its off-flavor. The high temperature also stops the enzyme activity that continued after frying. As a result, the fermentation of the tea stops. After drying, the tea ends up with a special roasted aroma.
